## 1. The preregistered comparison is degenerate at the primary setting (a planning error)

The plan fixed coverage levels {{0.50, 0.70, 0.85}} assuming a near-oracle producer. At the **primary setting (mention-miss 0.2)** neither family reaches even 0.50 coverage: the best flat merger tops out at {cov_flat_m02} and the graph at {cov_graph_m02}. The matched comparison is therefore undefined there, and the mechanical rule returns STOP for a reason that says nothing about graphs: there was no useful region. It is recorded as found; the preregistered decision is `{outcome}`, with the graph better at {levels_better} of 3 levels under every controller mode.
Why coverage is capped: the controller needs switch states and gate requirements, T1 is the only producer that proposes them, and a missed mention removes the fact. **No merger can supply a fact nobody proposed.**

## 3. Mechanisms: what carried anything

Ablations at the primary setting (maximum coverage and harm at the most permissive operating point, defer controller):

{ablation_table}

- **Multi-producer agreement is the one mechanism with a large effect** (without it the graph loses {agree_loss} points of coverage at the controlled-low setting). But the **calibrated flat merger recovers the same thing**: a logistic regression over `[logit(confidence), emitted]` per producer is a learned sum of evidence, which is what naive-Bayes agreement is. The graph adds nothing over it.
- **Functional location slots, contradiction handling, slot and link completion and relation semantics moved the operating point along the same frontier (more coverage with more harm, or the reverse) without improving it**; removing relation semantics even *raised* coverage at the strict end of the controlled settings.
- **Level 1 (edge quality) and level 2 (graph consistency) do change** — the structural steps lower the rate of entities with two kept locations — **but that did not become controller utility.** A better-behaved graph was not a better decision.

## 4. What this does and does not say

- It says: with a fixed controller and these producers, arbitrating uncertain propositions **as graph edges** bought nothing over arbitrating them as scores, once the score merger is allowed to learn producer weights. The graph remains a fine representation (X0); there is no evidence here that graph-aware arbitration is what to build.
- It does **not** say graphs cannot help: the producers here were weak where the controller is demanding (states and gates), the structure available to exploit was thin (one location per entity, one state per switch, a connected chain), and the producer was a regular-expression extractor on template text. A richer relation ecology (BANK-v2) is where structure could matter; that is V2-2's question, and the X1 result lowers the prior that it will.
- **X5 is not earned.** The plan adds NLI as a producer only if X1 continues. It does not, so NLI was not started.

## Limits

Exploratory and development-grade. T1 is a simple extractor with a synthetic mention-miss factor, not a learned observer; T2 is cached (its model is no longer on disk); T3 is synthetic; BANK-v1's label conventions set floors (the evaluation subset removes them by construction). The preregistered levels were unreachable at the primary setting, and the exploratory levels were chosen after seeing that. Bootstrap intervals are over worlds within one fold.
