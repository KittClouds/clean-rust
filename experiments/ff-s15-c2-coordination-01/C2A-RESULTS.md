# C2a results — do the four surfaces find different safe actions?

**Outcome under the preregistered rule: NO. Oracle headroom at α = 0.05 is 15.3% (bar: 25%), so C2b is not earned and C2 stops here.**
CAL half of DEV only, C1's frozen thresholds, no policy trained or scored, HOLD untouched. Generated by `tools/make_results.py` from `results/c2a-census.json`
(plan: `C2A-PLAN.md`, written before the census was run).

## The headline numbers (α = 0.05, CAL, 11890 rows, 5531 of them truth-ACT)

| | correctly executed | share of true-ACT rows |
|---|---|---|
| primary surface (`middle_plus_final`) alone | 740 | 13.4% |
| **union** of all four surfaces (an oracle that picks the right one per row) | 853 | 15.4% |
| intersection (all four agree and are right) | 0 | — |

The union finds only 113 rows the primary misses (15.3% more). `full_mean` fits no ACT rule at this α, so it contributes nothing to the union;
the three routers overlap heavily: pairwise Jaccard of their safe sets is 0.53–0.54, and where they differ the primary usually holds the larger share
(e.g. against `final_plus_mean` the primary alone is right on 305 rows and the other alone on 88).
This is an **upper bound**: a real policy has no oracle. It cannot know which surface to trust on a row, and 43 of the union's 896 executed rows have a harmful qualifier.

## Why: the surfaces make the same mistakes (exploratory, post hoc)

The veto census in the plan asked whether another surface's frozen ABSTAIN rule would block the primary's harmful executions. It would not: **0 of the primary's 28
harmful executions are vetoed by any other surface, and 4 correct ones would be lost.** An ABSTAIN veto has no measurable benefit at these operating points.

That prompted a look (added after the planned census was read, so treat it as exploratory) at what the other surfaces say on those 28 rows. Of them, 22 are acts on rows whose truth is ABSTAIN,
3 on ASK rows and 3 are wrong-action errors. On those same rows `final_plus_mean` says ACT on 28 of 28, `layer_m4_final` on 26 and `full_mean` on 26,
with a median P(ABSTAIN) of only 6.2%–13.2%. **The four views of the one frozen backbone are confidently wrong together.** Coordination among views cannot repair an error that every view shares, which is
what the small headroom and the useless veto both say.

## Sensitivity (descriptive; the go/no-go rule was fixed at α = 0.05 and is not revisited)

| α | primary correct (share of true-ACT) | union correct (share) | oracle headroom | pessimistic union harm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | 236 (4.3%) | 493 (8.9%) | 108.9% | 1.0% |
| 0.05 | 740 (13.4%) | 853 (15.4%) | 15.3% | 4.8% |
| 0.10 | 991 (17.9%) | 1342 (24.3%) | 35.4% | 11.3% |
| 0.20 | 2188 (39.6%) | 2898 (52.4%) | 32.4% | 23.9% |

Looser α shows more relative headroom (α = 0.10: 35.4%), but only by operating at a higher harm rate, and it remains an oracle bound. The absolute numbers stay small.

## A warning for any future coordination gate (exploratory, post hoc)

The relative gate proposed for C2b — harm ≤ 7% and a ≥ 25% gain over C1's α = 0.05 point — is **all but met by one surface with no coordination at all**, just by loosening α: +24% at 6.5% harm (alpha 0.08), and +29% at 7.3% (alpha 0.09).
No grid point clears both bars, but the gap is a rounding error. Refitting the primary's ACT rule on CAL at intermediate α (CAL is in-sample here, so real harm would be a point or two higher):

| α | ACT threshold | correct-ACT share | vs α = 0.05 | CAL harm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 855000 | 13.4% | +0% | 3.6% |
| 0.06 | 850000 | 13.9% | +4% | 4.3% |
| 0.07 | 840000 | 15.2% | +14% | 5.3% |
| 0.08 | 830000 | 16.5% | +24% | 6.5% |
| 0.09 | 825000 | 17.2% | +29% | 7.3% |
| 0.10 | 820000 | 17.9% | +34% | 7.9% |

So a coordination policy has to be judged **frontier against frontier at matched harm** — its harm-versus-coverage curve must dominate the single-surface curve — not against one point of it.

## What this means

- The black-box theory's "several cheap views → coordination" step is **not supported** for these four views: they are largely redundant and share their errors. C2b is not built.
- What does help is unchanged from C1: confidence-gated routing on the primary surface. Its useful range on this data ends around α = 0.10 (about 17.9% of true-ACT rows at about 7.9% CAL harm); looser points buy more coverage only at double-digit harm, unless the observers themselves get better.
- Views that fail *independently* would be needed for coordination to pay — different substrate or view families rather than more surfaces of the same backbone. That is a question for the representation ladder, not for a controller rule.
- ASK is still untouched, as planned. It gets its own rung.
- Everything here is DEV evidence. The fresh sealed split is what would turn any of it into a confirmatory claim.
