# C4a results — action-conditional safety census

**Outcome under the preregistered gate: no. Perfect action-specific thresholds would add only +7.3% at 3% harm and +7.6% at 5% on the primary surface (bar 10% at both, above the noise band), so C4b is not earned and C4 stops.**
The gate fails on all four surfaces. CAL half of DEV only, no policy, no training, no HOLD, no TEST. Generated by `tools/make_results.py` from `results/c4a-census.json` (plan: `PLAN.md`, written before any per-action safety was computed).

**But the census found something more useful than an oracle gain: the cheap tier is a NOOP executor.** A single confidence threshold already draws the applicability boundary, and it draws it at "do nothing".

## The gate (primary surface, `middle_plus_final`)

Eligible actions (≥ 300 candidates and ≥ 100 safe): MOVE, ACTIVATE, NOOP; 112 REQUEST candidates are excluded from both arms. The oracle is the exact per-action optimum (dynamic programming, verified against brute force in the tests).

| harm target H | global C1 threshold: correct executions | per-action oracle | oracle gain | noise band p95 (shuffled actions) | passes (≥ 10% and above noise) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3% | 683 | 733 | +7.3% | +9.5% | (gate level) no |
| 5% | 819 | 881 | +7.6% | +7.0% | (gate level) no |
| 7% | 927 | 1051 | +13.4% | +9.5% | not a gate level |
| 10% | 1116 | 1454 | +30.3% | +14.2% | not a gate level |

The gain is small exactly where the controller operates and large only at 10% harm — the same loose-end pattern as C3a. At 10% the oracle's answer is essentially "execute NOOP, at a lower confidence threshold" (1612 of its 1615 executions are NOOP); it spends almost no harm budget on MOVE and none on ACTIVATE.

## What the cheap tier actually executes

C1's frozen α = 0.05 rule on the primary surface, by the action it proposes:

| predicted action | executed | correct | harmful | share of truth-ACT rows correctly executed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NOOP | 735 | 715 | 20 | 25.9% of 2756 |
| MOVE | 31 | 24 | 7 | 1.2% of 1946 |
| ACTIVATE | 2 | 1 | 1 | 0.1% of 829 |

96% of what the acting tier executes is NOOP — a correct "nothing needs doing" — and it correctly executes **0.9% of the truth MOVE and ACTIVATE rows** (25 of 2775), against 25.9% of the truth NOOP rows.
This is the honest reading of C1's "5.7% of rows get a correct execution": it is almost entirely NOOP. The same holds on every surface that executes anything:

| surface | executed (NOOP / MOVE / ACTIVATE) | correct (NOOP / MOVE / ACTIVATE) | NOOP share of executions |
|---|---|---|---|
| middle_plus_final | 735 / 31 / 2 | 715 / 24 / 1 | 96% |
| final_plus_mean | 532 / 4 / 1 | 518 / 4 / 1 | 99% |
| layer_m4_final | 460 / 1 / 0 | 445 / 1 / 0 | 100% |
| full_mean | none (no ACT rule at alpha 0.05) | – | – |

At the *same* global threshold, harm differs enormously by action (primary surface, threshold for H = 5%):

| predicted action | executed | harmful | harm rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACTIVATE | 3 | 2 | 66.7% |
| MOVE | 44 | 10 | 22.7% |
| NOOP | 815 | 31 | 3.8% |

So the assumption behind one global threshold — `P(harm | confidence, action)` the same for every action — is false; the action carries the harm. It just does not matter to the oracle, because the global threshold already keeps almost every MOVE and ACTIVATE out.

## How safe is each action's very best candidate? (post hoc)

Harm rate among the k highest-confidence candidates predicted as each action (added after the conditional-harm table was read):

| surface | NOOP top 25 / 100 / 200 | MOVE top 25 / 100 / 200 | ACTIVATE top 25 / 100 / 200 |
|---|---|---|---|
| middle_plus_final | 0% / 0% / 0% | 20% / 35% / 37% | 24% / 39% / 38% |
| final_plus_mean | 0% / 0% / 0% | 24% / 28% / 32% | 36% / 33% / 31% |
| layer_m4_final | 0% / 0% / 0% | 36% / 44% / 43% | 48% / 42% / 40% |
| full_mean | 4% / 4% / 7% | 48% / 52% / 52% | 40% / 38% / 41% |

NOOP's best candidates are essentially never harmful. **MOVE and ACTIVATE are never safe at any confidence on any surface**: even each one's top 25 has at least 20% harm. Harm does fall toward the very top, but it never approaches the 3–5% that NOOP reaches.
That is why per-action thresholds have nothing to reallocate at 3–5% harm: there are no safe MOVE or ACTIVATE executions to find.

## What this means

- **The cheap tier has an applicability boundary, and it is already implicit.** It is applicable to NOOP and, in practice, to nothing else. A per-action rule would add nothing to what one confidence threshold already does.
- **C1's coverage number needs re-reading.** Truth ACT on CAL is NOOP 2756, MOVE 1946, ACTIVATE 829. Roughly half of the ACT rows (50% MOVE or ACTIVATE) will escalate at *any* threshold that keeps harm near 5%, because the observers cannot separate safe from unsafe among them.
  That fixes the shape of the economics rung (C7): the larger tier must handle every real action, and the cheap tier saves only the "do nothing" decisions.
- **Coherence check:** the 112 candidates whose predicted action can never be a correct ACT execution (REQUEST) are all excluded here, and C1's frozen rule executed **none** of them, so a tighter granted-action set would have changed nothing at C1's operating point.
- **Not a rescue.** No per-action rule was earned or built; this is a description of what the existing controller does.

## Limits

CAL only; thresholds and the oracle are in-sample (optimistic in the oracle's favour, which makes the null stronger). The post-hoc tables are descriptive and were added after the conditional-harm table was read. The eligibility bar (300 candidates, 100 safe) excludes REQUEST and DEACTIVATE;
no action outside NOOP, MOVE and ACTIVATE is ever a truth ACT action in BANK-v1 (TRAIN counts in `c4/common.py`). Development evidence; HOLD unused; the fresh sealed split request stays queued.
