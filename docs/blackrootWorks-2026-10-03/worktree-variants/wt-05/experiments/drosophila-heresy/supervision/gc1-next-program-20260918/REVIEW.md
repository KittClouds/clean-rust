# Review of the GC1 follow-up recommendation

**Disposition: proceed with diagnostic planning; do not preselect geometry as the solution.**

The supplied response accurately carries forward the audited partial result: all 14 saved valid outputs improve baseline, aggregate mismatches fall 27.53%, and zero exact alternative endpoints exist. Its one-seed caveat and insistence on unchanged final gates are correct. The response supports a useful next program, with the following refinements.

## Material refinements

1. **Keep receipt anatomy separate from intervention.** D1 reconstructs saved states and signed geometry. Replacing a group with ZERO or another palette entry creates a new endpoint and requires a separately frozen replay identity. Support overlap is not causal attribution.
2. **Preserve identity meanings.** GC2 already denotes fresh-engineering-state constructor qualification in this program. Use GC1-GA1 for a geometry-aware development successor; do not silently give GC2 another purpose.
3. **Define cancellation as replacement.** Exactly one choice per group remains mandatory. Single/pair corrections replace existing choices relative to the original baseline. Two alternatives for one group cannot be combined.
4. **Separate linear geometry from nonlinear readout.** Axis and linear-drive changes have signed additive coordinate accounting under the verified mappings. Squared norm supports coordinate decomposition; norm itself does not. Sequential-f32 readout needs actual joint replay. These are different calculations.
5. **Define a useful correction concretely.** Require complete final validity and a global lexicographic score better than that case's historical best-valid output. Report how much of the invalid state's mismatch advantage survives descriptively. Avoid an undefined 'most of the advantage' gate.
6. **Bound the claim, not just the computation.** Exhaustive singles can rule out success only in the one-group replacement neighborhood. A screened pair failure rules out neither omitted pairs nor a different construction trajectory. Do not infer palette infeasibility or abandon geometry-aware assembly from that result alone.
7. **Compare a successor fairly.** A new beam policy needs a common-runtime control with the same palette, ordering, horizon, final gates and budget. A four-lane bundled policy can be useful without establishing which lane caused its benefit.

## A concrete new source finding

The inspected PAR8 code computes exploration buckets as `round(debt * 4096)`, where debt is normalized signed axis error, normalized signed norm error, and unsigned linear-error norm. Its bucket width is approximately 2.44e-4. Final-valid errors are bounded by 2e-6, 2e-7 and 2e-6 respectively. Therefore every final-valid state lies in the same geometry bucket, before active-group count is included.

This is a verified property of the code, not a measured explanation of search failure. Historical unretained beam states are unavailable to this review. D1 should quantify the representational resolution and saved-state assignments; a future policy should qualify gate-unit bins and signed linear vectors if it claims geometry diversity.
